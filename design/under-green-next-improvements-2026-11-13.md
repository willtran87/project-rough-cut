# Under the Green: next experience improvements

These are proposals, not additional implemented features. The current pass delivers the faster scythe attack, three illustrated opening panels, and an illustrated defeat screen. Preserve E interaction, Q rear glance, full-level retries, physical pursuit, and sound-led feedback.

## 1. Make a missed cut leave evidence

When the blade physically hits masonry, emit a short directional scrape, a few dirty droplets and a shallow temporary mark at the contact point. Let a water strike throw a low fan of muddy spray. Use actual blade contact, not a scripted effect in front of the camera. Pool a small fixed number of marks and particles. This makes Joe's reach understandable and gives a narrow escape a satisfying finish without announcing it in the banner. Do not add flashes, hit-stop or camera shake by default.

Acceptance: effects occur only at genuine contacts; the missed strike still grants the existing recovery window; Reduced Motion suppresses disruptive particles; no per-frame resource allocation. Local scythe effects, using a shared material/contact convention where other levels need it.

## 2. Give the pump return a distinct audible approach

Build a short sequence from Joe's real route: shoe on service grating, a scythe scrape at a turn, then wet steps entering the gallery. Each sound follows his actual location and physical surface. Preserve his faster unseen travel, but never play a false positional warning or let a flourish cover the attack cue. A player who listens should be able to anticipate the direction without knowing the exact arrival time.

Acceptance: left/right and wall muffling agree with his location; the final approach remains a slow visible walk; the sequence cannot repeat every few seconds. Spatial sound conventions should be global; this authored encounter is local.

## 3. Let flood pressure create a readable route choice

Before a whole-level rise, use pipe knocks, a moving waterline and debris lifting from the floor. Provide a short raised edge that avoids the worst slowdown but costs distance and exposes the player to Joe's sightline. Keep every required crossing escapable under the existing attack warning and recovery timings. Do not turn flood onset into an unavoidable capture when the player is already committed to a channel.

Acceptance: test onset at each crossing entry and each pump interaction phase; verify walking and reduced-motion play; no reliance on a banner or blue glowing water. Local geometry and flood tuning; shared hazard-warning principles.

## 4. Give one ghost encounter a narrative purpose

Have a distant shadow walk past the keeper-room doorway carrying its scythe, fade around the bend, and leave the doorway empty. An optional line in the groundskeeper's record can echo its route or shift. Use the existing moving/fading apparition system and collision-safe paths. Keep appearances sparse and never substitute a ghost for Joe's lethal attack silhouette at close range.

Acceptance: continuous walking through both fades; no teleport, stationary materialization, or mandatory jump scare. The apparition setting disables it. Local narrative, shared apparition preferences.

## 5. Strengthen the last stretch through contrast

After the bypass operates, briefly reduce the machinery bed so footsteps carry, then let the final sluice mechanism supply the noise and urgency. Keep the escape interaction physically understandable: visible travel of the door and a readable remaining gap. The payoff should be the room becoming quieter as the door closes, followed by the record's connection to the eighteenth hole. No added checkpoint or action announcement.

Acceptance: the quieter interval does not remove real threat cues; repeated attempts can proceed immediately; dialogue/art cannot lock player input during danger. Local encounter staging.

## Global presentation contract

Keep shared intro controls, skip/replay behavior, image-failure fallback, reduced-motion support and full-image responsive framing consistent across chapters. Each chapter owns its art, narrative and restart rules. Under the Green always restarts at its entrance. Prefer these reusable presentation controls over copying another independent slideshow implementation.

Priority: missed-cut contact feedback first, then the pump approach, then flood route choice. Test the new attack with players before increasing lethality further; the visible windup and escape opportunity are essential to making fear feel earned.
