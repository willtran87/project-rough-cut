# The Back Nine — shed branch verification

## Implemented

Win Level 1 through the shed and choose **Drive the Back Nine**, or open http://127.0.0.1:4187/back-nine.html directly. Drive the golf cart through both lit shelters, then brake inside the clubhouse service garage. Joe pursues on a three-deck industrial riding mower. Three alternate paths, wet turf, bunkers and visible obstacles give route choices. Checkpoints and completed chapters persist locally; a fresh shed win resets this chapter. The drain retains its existing ending.

Controls: W/Up accelerate, A/D or arrows steer, S/Down brake/reverse, Space brake, hold Q rear view, Escape pause, F fullscreen. Controller: left stick, RT accelerate, LT brake/reverse, B brake, R3 rear, Start pause. Coarse-pointer devices get touch buttons.

Level 1 is unloaded before loading this page. Joe uses original face, hair and clothing image bytes on the lightweight rig. `tools/build-joe-rider.py` reproducibly builds the 6,340,664-byte rider GLB without the old mower. No underground Level 2 or Level 3 is claimed.

## Verification

- `verify-back-nine.mjs`: acceleration, brake/reverse, wet/sand handling, visible boundaries/garage collisions, pause, remembered-position pursuit, A* around the clubhouse, contact/catch and checkpoint reset. Six ordinary-control simulated wins: main route (~63 seconds), three alternatives (~63–64.4 seconds), both checkpoint starts. Joe moves continuously; closest alternate-route approach was 5.5 m. Automated steering is not a human difficulty test.
- `verify-back-nine-browser.cjs`: keyboard, pointer driving, virtual gamepad polling, rear view, pause/resume, fullscreen, checkpoint reload, completion and completed reload, replay and caught retry. Controlled placements isolate triggers and rendering. Physical controller feel is not assessed.
- Inspected full-page captures at 2560x1600, 1280x720 and 844x390, including fork, rear mower, wet turf/spray, shelter, garage, briefing and pause. No new browser errors.
- `verify-back-nine-transition.cjs`: drain retains rematch; shed result loads the chapter and resets its checkpoint. Existing scoring occurs before navigation.
- `verify-win-objectives.cjs`: all three Level 1 orders x both exits still win, including no optional lure balls and canceled/restarted filing.
- Installed standard client via `verify-presented-client.mjs`: two input bursts, 16.7 m forward progress, steering/braking and Joe pursuing. State and presented screenshot inspected; no error artifacts.
- Release audit passes 90 action fixtures. Its existing image scanner does not count GLB payloads.

## Performance evidence and limits

Opaque chunk batches, fixed spray pool, three persistent audio voices, one local light, no shadows/full-screen postprocessing, bounded A* at approximately 0.65-second intervals and one outstanding GPU fence. Textures decode cooperatively; shaders warm before Start. Idle briefing/pause stops repeated drawing. Blur/hidden pauses both simulation and audio. Software mode uses the existing discrete resolution budget.

These are local diagnostic samples. Fresh browser contexts can still benefit from warm OS/driver caches. Completion samples include the small fence polling delay and controlled wet/rear-view checks. They do not establish a causal improvement over Level 1, universal 60 FPS, cold OS startup, or zero interference with other graphics applications. Repeated rear/wet views hold at 10 programs and 13 textures. Reports/screenshots are in `output/back-nine/`.

## Human playtest prepared for later

1. Win through the shed. Check that the continuation, narrative and first objective need no explanation.
2. Drive a fresh run without a map. Record the first unclear bend, surface or shelter.
3. Try each fork. Assess whether the choice matters and Joe's cuts/turns look believable.
4. Get caught after a shelter, retry and reload. Assess recovery fairness.
5. Use rear view while steering and test wet turns. Rate camera comfort, brake/reverse feel and controller sensitivity.
6. Find the garage and stop inside. Check whether its warm light, instruction and closing shutter communicate success.
7. Listen on speakers/headphones at normal volume. Rate mower direction/approach, impact cue, mute and pause behavior.
8. Keep the other normally used graphics program open during loading and a chase. Record hardware, resolution and observed stalls.

Record input device, completion/catch location, fun/suspense/clarity/comfort (1–5), and the most frustrating moment. Human testing remains deferred by the user. Tune using these observations rather than treating automated driving as proof of fun.
