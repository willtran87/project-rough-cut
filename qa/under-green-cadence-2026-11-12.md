# Under the Green — walking cadence and moving clearance

Joe's walking cycle now covers 1.1 metres rather than 0.74 metres. The leg trajectory expands with that cycle, while world-space supports preserve planted feet. Slow turns use longer repositioning steps; fast turns retain shorter supports. A slight knee bend remains through stops and pivots, and an overextended walking support releases with a short lifted correction. Player controls and Joe's simulation speed, route, perception, turning and attack rules are unchanged.

The source clip's grounded-foot discontinuity was approximately 3 cm per loop after the initial stride expansion. Contact anchoring removes it. The first broader route test also exposed unreachable feet during walking restarts and rapid pivots, which isolated turn tests had missed. The corrective step and retained knee bend address those transitions without stretching bones or changing navigation.

Decorative bank stripes previously stood 4.5 cm above the walking plane and intersected Joe's shoes. Bank and route paint now top out at 2 mm, and drain grates sit flush. This is a local geometry correction; water timing and physical routes are unchanged.

## Verified evidence

- `verify-under-green-walking-supports.mjs` passes the grounded loop seam, duplicate update, stop handoff, overextended restart and time/position reset regressions. The deliberately stuck-low source shoe triggers a lifted correction instead of remaining anchored indefinitely.
- `verify-under-green-cadence.cjs --baseline` uses the preserved immediate pre-cadence actor and footwork sources in `output/under-green/cadence/before/`. It does not use the older pre-footwork reconstruction. The new test samples three walking speeds at 60, 30 and 15 presentation updates per second, measuring world-space ankle travel while both consecutive shoe-height samples are within 8 mm of the floor. At 2.1 m/s and 60 Hz, contacts fall from 16 to 11 per three seconds; grounded ankle median speed falls from about 0.053 m/s to below 0.000001 m/s. Final maximum across all nine cases is 0.0684 m/s, including low toe-off/landing frames. This is a sampled contact check, not a claim of zero motion during every part of foot roll.
- Final `verify-under-green-footwork.cjs`: 1,890 rendered states, duplicate-render equality, 2,023 planted comparisons and 318 actual skinned-vertex floor samples pass. Maximum target error is approximately 0.00000021 m; minimum sampled sole height is approximately 2 mm above the floor.
- `verify-under-green-moving-clearance.cjs --poses-only`: 3,366 rendered states from actual simulated dry, wet and final-checkpoint routes pass ankle/hand reach. Samples preserve 20 Hz presentation continuity. Maximum ankle target error is 2.14 mm on the wet route; the other two routes remain below 0.000001 m. Scripted pathfinding is not unfamiliar-player evidence.
- Final `verify-under-green-moving-clearance.cjs`: the same three routes pass 256 rendered body/tool-versus-triangle clearance samples (104 dry, 121 wet, 31 final-checkpoint), with no intersections or browser errors. These samples include regular one-second intervals and phase transitions, with extra checks at processing-chunk boundaries. They are not an every-frame swept-mesh proof.
- Final footstep-audio browser tests pass nine trajectories, actual pursuit, duplicate/idle/pause/mute/retry checks and independent simultaneous-contact envelopes. Three-second walk/slow-turn contact counts are 11 at 60/30 Hz and 10 at 15 Hz; actual pursuit produced eight cues. ## Cost and remaining acceptance

The refreshed isolated actor CPU profile, run after the other browser tests finished, measured median batch means of 0.1278 ms per walking update and 0.1310 ms per turning update. The reconstructed pre-footwork baseline measured 0.0570/0.0556 ms in that run. Each case uses twelve 500-update batches after warmup in Chrome 148 on this Windows host. This comparison includes all leg IK, sole constraints and contact tracking added since the older baseline, not just this cadence change. It excludes GPU, scene and audio work; batch means are not frame-time percentiles or phone-performance evidence. Current and historical reports are preserved in the cadence output directory.

This improves review items 18–20, 23, 31 and 44. Full trajectory coverage at every frame, continuous animation judgement, listening, physical devices, unfamiliar-player acceptance and the rest of the 44-item implementation objective remain open. Passing these engineering samples does not prove the chapter is fun, frightening or complete.
