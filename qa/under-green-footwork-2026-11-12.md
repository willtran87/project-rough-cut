# Under the Green — planted turns and sole clearance

Joe now keeps a supporting foot fixed in world space while turning or stopping, lifts the other for a short repositioning step, and blends back into his distance-driven walk. This is chapter-local presentation: his simulation position, speed, turn rate, navigation, perception and attack timing are unchanged. The original model, material maps and shared trimmer poses remain in use.

The baseline reused the forward walking clip for stationary turns. The diagnostic turn at 1.2 radians/second showed substantial ground-level foot travel as the body rotated. `tools/probe-under-green-footwork.cjs --baseline` reconstructs that earlier actor adapter for comparison; `before.json` and `before.png` retain its sampled poses. Low-ankle motion is a useful diagnostic, not a complete contact classifier.

`web/under-green-footwork.js` maintains planted supports, alternating 160 ms repositioning steps, a brief airborne-foot landing and a 120 ms return to walking. It anticipates only Joe's current body turn. This prevents a slow presentation rate from leaving the support target too far behind his hip. It resets after a discontinuous position/time change and does not advance during duplicate renders. Reduced motion suppresses idle decoration as before; necessary foot placement remains visible.

Leg IK reuses the existing two-bone solver. A separate floor constraint caches the model's rigid shoe vertices in foot coordinates and checks their transformed lower extent. The original walking clip dipped a heel approximately 5.8 mm below the floor in sampled poses; the correction lifts only the required ankle amount. Using an axis-aligned box initially overestimated the shoe's lower extent, so the final implementation uses the actual cached vertices. No new meshes or textures are added. Per-frame actor cost and long-session performance still require qualification.
