## Performance investigation

Added a diagnostic component profiler with valid on-floor gallery and keeper camera checks. It compares the full scene, omission of custom practical lighting, omission of the stain shader, an inexpensive untextured shader, and a larger occluder budget. These omissions change the image and cannot qualify as production optimizations. The first development run had an invalid keeper location and recreated its basic material each frame; both defects were corrected before the retained report. The corrected report is still **provisional for timing** because independently owned Final Cut verification/client processes overlapped it. The 96-occluder variant retained 376 gallery draw calls and removed only 2,474 submitted triangles, so a larger occluder budget is not a compelling next optimization.

Tested a separate static depth-prepass candidate without adding it to production. Four 960×600 gallery/keeper/bunker/pump comparisons are pixel-exact (zero changed channels). Its performance sample waits for other live render jobs to finish, then alternates enabled/disabled frames with GPU-fence completion at 480×300: 24 samples each after six paired warmups. Baseline median/p95: 76.1/79 ms. Candidate: 84/88 ms. **Rejected**: the extra pass increases cost despite image parity. Candidate source and report remain in tools/fixtures and output/under-green/visual-upgrade/pass14-depth for reference; the game does not import this module.

The isolated baseline confirms software performance remains below target. Next investigation should measure finer material/draw-group cost under isolated conditions before changing batching or lighting. Preserve all 17 fixed practical sources, physical solid-wall depth and the full high-resolution art target. Do not ship any diagnostic omission.

Artifacts: pass14-sign, pass14-profile, pass14-depth, pass14-source and pass14-client beneath output/under-green/visual-upgrade (existing junction preserved).

The two-line plate exposed a pre-existing material-label hazard: `material.name` copied label newlines, and Three injects that name into a GLSL `#define`. If this material compiles first for a shared program, the continuation becomes invalid shader code. Sign material names now collapse whitespace while the illustrated texture retains its intended line breaks. The blank intermediate plate was caught by screenshot review and console-error assertions; it is not accepted evidence. This also protects other multiline drain signs independently of compilation order.
